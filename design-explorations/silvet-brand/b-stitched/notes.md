# B · Stitched — running seam

**Hypothesis:** Let the S do the naming; let one quiet line of running stitches do the remembering. The seam is cut as a true transparent channel through the ribbon and rebuilt with short, rounded thread marks. It reads as a crafted construction detail rather than a dotted perimeter, needle, or badge.

The main mark is a single, independently drawn filled silhouette. Its broad, continuous turns and open counters keep the S legible before the stitch detail registers. The upper and lower terminals taper into the body with restrained, Instrument Serif-like contrast; no type glyph or other font geometry is used. The silhouette leaves a small, even margin within the 64-unit square.

## Color and use

- On Warm Chalk (`#F4F0E9`), set the mark to Midnight Aubergine (`#211A2B`).
- On Midnight Aubergine (`#211A2B`), set the mark to Warm Chalk and recolor only `[data-role="detail"]` to Electric Citron (`#D9F36A`). The body stays Chalk.
- For true single-ink use, keep the body and detail the same `currentColor`; the seam remains coherent because the gaps are actual transparency, not a painted background.
- No Lilac is used. The compact symbol is intended to sit beside the existing Instrument Serif uppercase SILVET wordmark without competing with its generous tracking.

## Small-size tradeoff

The primary S is deliberately much heavier than its stitch. At 32px the running seam should read clearly; at 24px it becomes a subtle atelier cue. At 16px the main silhouette remains the priority and individual stitches may merge or soften with rasterization—an honest loss of detail rather than an artificially enlarged dotted outline. Inspect at **16px, 24px, and 32px**, especially the silhouette and whether the seam still feels subordinate at 16px.
